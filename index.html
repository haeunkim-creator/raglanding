The problem

Nobody can tell you where a financial answer came from.

A model reads a filing, flattens the tables, loses the footnote that qualified the number, and produces a confident paragraph. The output looks right. Nobody downstream can check it, and in financial work an unverifiable answer is not a usable one.

Proof

Every step is on the screen while it runs.

Lucy answers from retrieved filing text and shows the whole path as it happens — the stages that ran, every chunk that came back, the passage each statement rests on, and a score on whether the answer is actually supported by it.

  1. 01

    The path

    Pipeline Graph and Trace Log show the route the question took and every chunk that was retrieved, as it runs rather than after the fact.

  2. 02

    The source

    The passage an answer rests on is highlighted where it sits in the filing. No opening EDGAR, no downloading anything.

  3. 03

    The score

    An LLM judge runs on each response in real time.

    • RelevanceHow well the answer corresponds to the question.
    • Retrieval RelevanceHow well the retrieved chunks correspond to it.
    • GroundednessWhether the response is factually supported by the source context; in effect, hallucination detection.

Loaded chunks are also browsable on their own — filtered by filing item, as text or table units.

Why it works

EDGAR is free. Making it retrievable is not.

Pulling filings is the easy part. The difficulty is everything that happens before retrieval — and it is where most financial RAG quietly breaks.

What goes wrong

  • 01 Fixed-length chunks split a statement mid-row.
  • 02 A chunk arrives with no record of its filing.
  • 03 A long item has to be read whole or not at all.
  • 04 Tables flatten into unlabeled runs of numbers.
  • 05 A cross-reference dead-ends at the mention.
  • 06 Charts and diagrams are dropped on the way in.
  • 07 Tagged financial values arrive as plain prose.
  • 08 Exhibits and attachments are skipped as boilerplate.
  • 09 Cover pages and signature blocks compete with real content.
  • 10 Extraction errors surface only when a user hits one.

What Lucy does

  • 01

    Item-level separation

    Body text is split by filing item, so chunk boundaries follow the document's own structure.

  • 02

    Document metadata

    Metadata ships with every chunk for search and filtering.

  • 03

    Summaries

    Item-level and document-level summaries are generated alongside the source text.

  • 04

    Table structure

    Tables are structured as JSON. Irregular layouts, including merged cells, are recognized rather than flattened.

  • 05

    Reference resolution

    Internal and external references carry the information needed to reach the referenced document.

  • 06

    Images

    Image content is described, not discarded.

  • 07

    XBRL

    Figures in the financial statements carry their XBRL information.

  • 08

    Attachments

    Exhibits and financial statements attached to the filing are provided.

  • 09

    Boilerplate handling

    Cover pages, signature blocks, and similar non-content are separated out.

  • 10

    QA

    Data quality is maintained through a review pass on the output.

What you can ask

Two kinds of question, one retrieval surface.

Financial statement data is normalized and loaded into a relational database, so questions that need filtering, aggregation, sorting, and comparison resolve against structured facts. Narrative sections are handled by vector search. Hybrid search covers statements and footnotes together.

RDB

Filtering, aggregation, sorting, and comparison over normalized financialstatement.json.

Questions with a complete answer set — every match, not a sample.

Vector search

Narrative sections of the filing.

Risk factors, business description, management discussion.

Lucy Intelligence

Interpretation, assessment, and forward-looking context.

Layered on top of retrieved filing data, not generated in place of it.

“Are there cheaper alternatives to VOO with similarly low expense ratios and comparable assets under management?”

Similarity search

  • Returns passages that read like the question.
  • A near-match is indistinguishable from a match.
  • One omission makes the answer wrong.

Structured retrieval

  • Filters, aggregates, and sorts across filings that match the conditions.
  • Completeness is a property of the query, not of the ranking.
  • Every filing that matches is returned, not the closest few.

Also: Show all US large-cap ETFs with expense ratios below 0.10%.

These describe what the data model supports. Scope and availability are confirmed per dataset.

Integration

The same retrieval, called from your own stack.

MCP

MCP connects Lucy's filing search to any MCP-capable host. rag_context(question, thread_id?) returns roughly 20 source chunks with scores and filing references, and the trace arrives as progress notifications while the call runs. Pass the returned thread_id on the next call for follow-ups.

The MCP endpoint returns chunks only — the calling model composes the answer.
