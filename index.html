What Lucy is not

Lucy is upstream of all four.

It supplies the filing evidence they answer from.

Not a chatbot — a retrieval layer your product calls.

Not an answer engine — retrieval-ready chunks your LLM answers from.

Not a terminal — structured filing data your systems consume.

Not a generation layer — chunks, scores, and references. Your LLM composes the final answer.

The problem

Most financial RAG failure starts before retrieval.

A model reads a filing, flattens the tables, loses the footnote that qualified the number, and produces a confident paragraph. The output looks right. Nobody downstream can check it, and in financial work an unverifiable answer is not a usable one. The failure happened upstream, in how the document was read.

Why it works

Preprocessing determines retrieval quality.

EDGAR is free. Making it retrievable is not. Pulling filings is the easy part — the difficulty is everything that happens before retrieval.

What goes wrong

  • 01 Fixed-length chunks split a statement mid-row.
  • 02 A chunk arrives with no record of its filing.
  • 03 A long item has to be read whole or not at all.
  • 04 Tables flatten into unlabeled runs of numbers.
  • 05 A cross-reference dead-ends at the mention.
  • 06 Charts and diagrams are dropped on the way in.
  • 07 Tagged financial values arrive as plain prose.
  • 08 Exhibits and attachments are skipped as boilerplate.
  • 09 Cover pages and signature blocks compete with real content.
  • 10 Extraction errors surface only when a user hits one.

What Lucy does

  • 01

    Item-level separation

    Body text is split by filing item, so chunk boundaries follow the document's own structure.

  • 02

    Document metadata

    Metadata ships with every chunk for search and filtering.

  • 03

    Summaries

    Item-level and document-level summaries are generated alongside the source text.

  • 04

    Table structure

    Tables are structured as JSON. Irregular layouts, including merged cells, are recognized rather than flattened.

  • 05

    Reference resolution

    Internal and external references carry the information needed to reach the referenced document.

  • 06

    Images

    Image content is described, not discarded.

  • 07

    XBRL

    Figures in the financial statements carry their XBRL information.

  • 08

    Attachments

    Exhibits and financial statements attached to the filing are provided.

  • 09

    Boilerplate handling

    Cover pages, signature blocks, and similar non-content are separated out.

  • 10

    QA

    Data quality is maintained through a review pass on the output.

What Lucy retrieves

Two kinds of retrieval, one surface.

One Vector & Graph RAG dataset: financial statement data is normalized and loaded into a relational database, so retrieval that needs filtering, aggregation, sorting, and comparison resolves against structured facts. Narrative sections are handled by vector search. Hybrid search covers statements and footnotes together, so one call returns graph/vector retrieval context.

one retrieval call

RDB

Filtering, aggregation, sorting, and comparison over normalized financialstatement.json. Every match, not a sample.

Vector search

Narrative sections of the filing — risk factors, business description, management discussion.

Layered on both

+ Graph — reference links between filings and the documents they cite: footnotes, exhibits, and cross-filing references.

Lucy Intelligence — interpretation, assessment, and forward-looking context — is a separate product, not part of the retrieval layer. Ask us if you need it.

“Are there cheaper alternatives to VOO with similarly low expense ratios and comparable assets under management?”

Similarity search

  • Returns passages that read like the question.
  • A near-match is indistinguishable from a match.
  • One omission makes the answer wrong.

Structured retrieval

  • Filters, aggregates, and sorts across filings that match the conditions.
  • Completeness is a property of the query, not of the ranking.
  • Every filing that matches is returned, not the closest few.

Also: Show all US large-cap ETFs with expense ratios below 0.10%.

These describe what the data model supports. Scope and availability are confirmed per dataset.

The playground exposes the retrieval itself — the stages that ran, every chunk that came back, the passage each statement rests on, and a score on whether the material actually supports it. It generates an answer so you can judge whether the retrieved material held it up. That generation is the test, not the product. What follows is a single query, end to end.

    The score

    The playground generates one answer only so it can be scored against the retrieved chunks — three metrics, in real time.

    • Relevance:How well the answer corresponds to the question.
    • Retrieval Relevance:How well the retrieved chunks correspond to it.
    • Groundedness:Factually supported by the source context; in effect, hallucination detection.
